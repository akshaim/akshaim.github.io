I lead software-defined vehicle (SDV) architecture at Analog Devices in Munich, working with technologies such as GMSL, E2B, A2B and wBMS. I come from product engineering, with hands-on experience in RTOS-based firmware (Zephyr, FreeRTOS, ChibiOS, RIOT-OS) and Linux kernel development, mainly in C and Rust. Today I connect stakeholders and engineering teams so that customers get one consistent journey, from initial integration and end-of-line (EOL) testing to start of production (SoP).

In Germany, I first joined the RIOT-OS core team in Berlin, where I helped scale the build system across several sensors and boards and wrote a low-level driver for wireless applications. I then became one of the maintainers of the software at STABL Energy, where I developed the build system, the embedded DevOps infrastructure and several low-level drivers, and introduced release management. After that I joined a product research team at Analog Devices, working on a new class of wireless communication products for automotive and industrial use.

In India, I worked with ICFOSS and the Kerala Startup Mission (KSUM) on building communities, and was part of the FOSSEE research team at the Aerospace department of IIT Bombay, where we developed platforms based on 4DIAC.

What I work on

  • Automotive and software-defined vehicles Software system architecture for automotive SDV platforms on embedded Linux and no-OS targets, around GMSL video links, Ethernet to the Edge Bus (E2B), the Automotive Audio Bus (A2B) and wireless battery management (wBMS).
  • Rust I use Rust for embedded firmware development and systems programming. I also work with communities on their move to Rust: see my talk From C Ops-Tables to Safe Rust Traits at the 1st Ariel OS Community Day in Grenoble, September 2026 (slides).
  • Embedded C, RTOS and firmware Kernel and driver development for real-time operating systems (RTOS), firmware build infrastructure with Kconfig and devicetree, and CI/CD for embedded targets.
  • Battery management and wireless Firmware and sensor integration for battery management systems (BMS), MAC and link-layer design for wireless battery management networks, and radio drivers.
  • Testing, safety and systems engineering I have been part of several platform bring-ups, which has meant owning the testbeds for hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) testing, virtual platforms in SystemC, and wireless network emulation platforms built on Renode and internal company tooling. On the systems engineering side, I work on requirements and architecture for safety-critical systems.
